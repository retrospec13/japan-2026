Trip thesis
City → coast → onsen

Start with Tokyo recovery, hit Osaka and Kyoto, then slow down in Mihama and Kinosaki before returning to Tokyo.

The clean route

Trip logic

  • Why Mihama? It gives you the quieter coastal-countryside feel without dragging the family all the way to Tomonoura.
  • Why Kinosaki? It is the easiest “classic onsen town” choice that still works with kids and the route.
  • Why not more Tokyo at the start? Because the first 48 hours after a long-haul flight should be for recovery, not trophy sightseeing.
  • Why one night in Osaka? Enough for Den Den Town and food without turning the trip into hotel-hopping.
  • Where is the slack? The third Tokyo night at the start is the flex day (Aug 24 open day), and the Sep 1 evening back in Ueno covers souvenirs before the 5 pm Sep 2 flight.

Tickets 101: read this first

The Welcome Suica. Red card, Mt. Fuji design. This plus cash is your whole payment strategy.
  1. At Narita, buy a Welcome Suica per adult. JR East Travel Service Center or the red Welcome Suica machines, arrivals floor of Terminal 1. No deposit, 28-day validity. Load ¥5,000 to start. Kids: ask at the JR counter for a child Suica (passport, half fares), or just buy kids paper tickets per ride.
  2. Tap it for everything local. Tokyo JR + Metro + subway, Osaka Metro, Kyoto subway and JR local lines, the Obama Line (ICOCA zone), konbini, vending machines. If it beeps, you are in.
  3. Three things it does NOT cover: the Keisei Skyliner (separate reserved ticket), any Shinkansen (separate ticket), and limited expresses like the Thunderbird (separate ticket). Buy those at station machines, ticket offices, or the apps below.
  4. Shinkansen tickets: SmartEX app (register your card before you fly) or the green JR ticket machines / any JR ticket office. Ueno and Tokyo Station both have them.
  5. Cash still matters: small-town taxis, the Tantetsu line on the Kinosaki leg, shrines, and market stalls. Keep ¥1,000 notes and coins on you.
Sat Aug 22

Narita T1 → Tabi Ueno

Land 3:10 pm. The only job: get into the city with the fewest decisions. Keisei Skyliner to Keisei-Ueno, then an 8-minute walk.

The Skyliner. White and blue, pointed nose. You board it in the basement of Narita T1.
  1. First job: the SIM. Stay on the arrivals floor (1F) and find the red JAL ABC counter — one in each wing, open 6:30 am until 1 hour after the last flight, so a 3:10 pm landing is easy. Show your Mobal order confirmation and passport, collect the SIM, load it before you leave the counter. (Bought the eSIM instead? Skip this stop — you are already online.)
  2. Then follow Railways / Train signs down to the basement. You want the Keisei Line side (sky blue), not the red JR side.
  3. Buy Skyliner tickets at the Keisei counter or blue machines: Narita Airport Terminal 1 → Keisei-Ueno. Adults about ¥2,580, kids half. Every seat reserved, car and seat number on the ticket.
  4. Ride 41 minutes to the last stop, Keisei-Ueno Station.
  5. Walk 8 minutes to Tabi Ueno, 2-19-6 Higashiueno. Save the pin offline. If bags are winning, a taxi from the station rank is about ¥700.
  6. Before 3:00 pm check-in? The hotel holds bags. Drop everything, go to the park.
Ticket: Keisei counter or blue machines at NRT T1, or ahead of time on the Keisei site. Suica does NOT cover the Skyliner seat charge.

Sat Aug 22

Ueno Park

The check-in-day decompression walk. Open space, zero commitment if the kids are wiped.

Ueno Park. Wide paths, fountains, shrine roofs in the trees.
  1. From Tabi Ueno, walk 10 minutes west toward JR Ueno Station. The park starts right behind the station.
  2. The landmark entrance is the Park Exit (Koen-guchi) side of JR Ueno Station. Fountains and the big open plaza are straight ahead.
  3. Keep it to a walk plus one stop. Zoo closes 5 pm, museums by 5:30. First evening is not the day for either.
Ticket: none. The park itself is free.

Mon Aug 24 · open Tokyo day

Tamagotchi Factory! (Harajuku)

The kids' store, on the open Tokyo day. Bring the Tamagotchi Paradise devices so they can connect at the in-store Lab Tama machine for the shop-only item.

Tokyu Plaza Harajuku "Harakado", the mirrored glass building at the Jingumae crossing. Tamagotchi Factory is on the 3rd floor.
  1. From JR Ueno Station, take the Yamanote Line (inner loop, toward Ikebukuro / Shinjuku) direct to Harajuku Station. About 25 minutes, ¥210, Suica.
  2. Exit Harajuku Station at the Omotesando Exit. Walk toward the big Jingumae intersection, about 4 minutes.
  3. Harakado is the mirrored-glass building on the corner. Tamagotchi Factory is on the 3rd floor. Address: 6-31-21 Jingumae, Shibuya-ku.
  4. Even faster exit if you end up on the Metro: Meiji-jingumae Station (Chiyoda / Fukutoshin lines), Exit 4 or 5, 1 minute to the building.
  5. Hours: 11:00 am to 9:00 pm. Also on site: Mini Tama All-You-Can-Pack (¥3,828) and custom patch-making (from ¥150).
Ticket: entry is free. Check tamagotchi-factory.jp the morning of, in case they bring back timed entry on busy days.
Pairs with HISEKI coin rings 3 minutes away on foot, and Meiji Shrine across the street if the adults want a decompression walk while the kids shop.

Tue Aug 25

Tabi Ueno → Tokyo Station → Shinkansen to Osaka

The long move south. Check out by 11 am, one JR hop to Tokyo Station, then the Tokaido Shinkansen to Shin-Osaka. About 2h21m on the fast train.

The Tokaido Shinkansen platform at Tokyo Station. White train, blue stripe. This is what you board.
  1. Check out of Tabi Ueno by 11:00 am. Walk 6 minutes to JR Ueno Station (or taxi ~¥700).
  2. Tap Suica in. Take any JR train toward Tokyo: Ueno-Tokyo Line, Yamanote, or Keihin-Tohoku. All of them hit Tokyo Station in about 7 minutes. ¥170.
  3. At Tokyo Station, follow the blue Tokaido Shinkansen signs toward the Yaesu side. Your gates are Yaesu North / Central.
  4. Board the Nozomi bound for Hakata. Get off at Shin-Osaka. About 2h21m.
  5. Sister rule stands: get there early, buy ekiben on the concourse BEFORE the platform hunt. The good boxes sell out by midday.
Ticket: about ¥14,720 per adult reserved, Nozomi. Buy on SmartEX or at the green JR machines / JR Central counter (Ueno or Tokyo Station). Kids 6-11 half. Any suitcase over 160 cm total (L+W+H) needs the free oversized-baggage seat reservation, book it when you buy.

Tue Aug 25

Shin-Osaka → Namba base

One red subway line, no transfers, 15 minutes. You sleep near Namba / Nipponbashi so the game run is a walk, not a project.

Shin-Osaka Station. You arrive on the Shinkansen level, the subway is below.
  1. At Shin-Osaka, follow signs for the Osaka Metro Midosuji Line (red M logo). It is the same building, just down the escalators.
  2. Tap Suica. Take the Midosuji Line toward Nakamozu.
  3. Ride about 15 minutes, no transfer, to Namba Station (M20). ¥240.
  4. For the Dotonbori / Nipponbashi side (Hotel K6 area), use the exits toward Dotonbori (exits 14-16 zone). Then it is a short walk.
Ticket: Suica tap, ¥240. Nothing to pre-buy.

Wed Aug 26

Super Potato · Den Den Town

The retro-game anchor of the Osaka morning. Timed window, one food stop, then out.

Den Den Town, Nipponbashi. Loud signage, game shops stacked on game shops. Super Potato sits on this strip.
  1. From the Namba hotel, walk 8-10 minutes north into Nipponbashi (Den Den Town). Flat, easy.
  2. Target: Super Potato Retro-kan, 3-8-18 Nipponbashi. Tall skinny building, retro game murals, you will hear the chiptunes.
  3. Skip-the-walk option: Osaka Metro Sakaisuji Line to Ebisucho Station, Exit 1-A, then 3 minutes on foot.
  4. Hours: weekdays 11:00-20:00, weekends 10:00-20:00. Open every day.
Ticket: none for the walk. ¥190 Suica tap if you ride to Ebisucho.
Set the budget at the door. Multiple floors of retro stock, and the kids will find the Pokemon glass case.

Namba → Kyoto Station

Afternoon move. Two taps, about an hour door to door, and you wake up Thursday already in Kyoto.

Kyoto Station front. Big glass grid, impossible to miss. Your hotel zone is around this building.
  1. From Namba (Midosuji Line, red), ride 8 minutes to Umeda Station. ¥190.
  2. Follow the covered walkways to JR Osaka Station (about 5 minutes underground, follow JR signs).
  3. Take the JR Kyoto Line Special Rapid (新快速) toward Kyoto / Maibara. 29 minutes to Kyoto Station. ¥580.
  4. Out at Kyoto Station, your station-area hotel is a short walk. Total from Namba: about 60 minutes with the transfer.
Ticket: all Suica, about ¥770 total. No reservations needed. If bags are heavy, coin lockers at Kyoto Station or check straight into the hotel.

Fushimi Inari Taisha

The temple block. Go early, walk a partial loop under the gates, leave before the heat wins.

JR Inari Station. Tiny, fox statues out front. The shrine gate is directly across the road from this building.
  1. From Kyoto Station, JR Nara Line (local, toward Nara). Track 8-10 zone.
  2. Ride 2 stops, 5 minutes, to Inari Station. ¥150 on Suica.
  3. Exit the station. The shrine's first big vermilion gate is right in front of you. No bus, no taxi, no navigation needed.
  4. Do the lower loop (the dense gate tunnels are in the first 30-40 minutes), then turn around when the kids are done.
Ticket: shrine is free, open dawn to dusk. Go before 8:30 am if you can.

Thu Aug 27

Nishiki Market

The food block. Covered arcade, graze-as-you-go lunch, good heat escape.

Nishiki Market. Covered arcade, packed stalls, yellow-green signage.
  1. From Kyoto Station, take the Karasuma Subway Line 2 stops to Shijo Station. ¥220.
  2. Walk 5 minutes west on Shijo, then turn into the covered arcade. Look for the Nishiki entrance sign between the storefronts.
  3. Flat-walk option: 25 minutes straight north from Kyoto Station if the weather is kind. It will not be. Take the subway.
Ticket: free to enter. Stalls are cash-friendly, keep ¥1,000s ready. Many close by 5-6 pm, so this is lunch, not dinner.
Some stalls frown on eat-while-walking. Buy, step to the side, finish, move on.

Fri Aug 28

Kyoto → Tsuruga → Mihama

The day the trip goes quiet. Limited express up the coast, then the little local line into Mihama.

The Obama Line platform at Tsuruga Station. Small local trains, one or two cars. This is your second train of the day.
  1. At Kyoto Station, buy Limited Express Thunderbird tickets to Tsuruga at the green JR West machines or ticket office. Reserved seats. About ¥4,500-5,000 per adult, confirm the exact fare in the JR West route search the night before.
  2. Ride the Thunderbird about 52 minutes to Tsuruga Station (the end of the line).
  3. Cross to the Obama Line (小浜線) platform, train bound for Higashi-Maizuru.
  4. Ride 21-24 minutes to Mihama Station (美浜). About ¥330, Suica works here.
  5. At Mihama: your inn picks you up if arranged, or the station taxi. Have the inn's phone number saved and cash ready.
Ticket: Thunderbird is a separate paper/app ticket, buy at Kyoto Station or JR West online. Obama Line leg is a Suica tap.
The Obama Line runs roughly hourly. Do not wing the connection. Check the exact transfer on JR West route search the night before and screenshot it.
Mihama Station. Small, one building, countryside quiet. When you see this, you have arrived.

Suishohama Beach

The kids' sea day and your "could I actually live here" scouting walk.

Suishohama Beach, Wakasa Bay. White sand, pine trees, no city.
  1. There is no station at the beach. From Mihama Station or your inn, it is a taxi, about 10 minutes, roughly ¥1,500.
  2. Ask the inn to call the cab the night before. Small-town taxis are not circling.
  3. Walking is possible (about 45 minutes, no bags) but in August heat with kids, take the taxi.
Ticket: none. Cash for the taxi, swim gear in a small beach bag.

Sun Aug 30

Mihama → Kinosaki Onsen

West along the coast to the onsen town. About 2h45-3h15 with two easy transfers.

Route note: this leg goes west: Obama Line to Maizuru, then the Tantetsu coastal line past Amanohashidate. There is no train from Tsuruga to Kinosaki, so do not route back east.
  1. From Mihama Station, take the Obama Line toward Higashi-Maizuru (NOT back toward Tsuruga). About 35 minutes. Suica works.
  2. At Higashi-Maizuru, ride one stop (3 minutes) to Nishi-Maizuru, or follow the station signs straight to the Tantetsu side.
  3. At Nishi-Maizuru, switch to the Kyoto Tango Railway (Tantetsu) Miyazu Line toward Toyooka. This leg runs past Amanohashidate, the pine-sandbar bay, which is the free scenic bonus of the day.
  4. Stay on through Toyooka to Kinosaki Onsen Station. Tantetsu trains run through onto the JR track for this last bit.
  5. Confirm the exact connection times at Mihama Station when you arrive on the 28th, or in JR West / Navitime search the night before. Local lines are hourly-ish.
Ticket: JR legs on Suica. The Tantetsu leg does NOT take IC cards, so buy a paper ticket at Nishi-Maizuru with cash (roughly ¥2,000 per adult).
Mon Aug 31

Kinosaki Onsen town

The slowest day of the trip. Baths, yukata walk, town snacks, second bath.

Kinosaki Onsen Station. Compact, wooden, one main street out front.
  1. The station is the top of town. The main onsen street runs straight out the front, 10 minutes on foot into the center.
  2. Most ryokan run pickup vans if you message an arrival time. Confirm with yours.
  3. The seven public bathhouses are all on that one street and its canal branch. Tattoos are OK at all seven per the town's official policy.
Ticket: bathhouses are pay-per-entry (about ¥700-900 each) or covered by a ryokan pass depending on your booking. Ask at check-in.

Tue Sep 1

Kinosaki → Tokyo (the long return)

Back to Tokyo the day before the flight so nothing ends on a tight connection. About 5 hours with one transfer.

Route note: the clean return is the direct limited express to Kyoto, then the Tokaido Shinkansen home. There is no practical Kinosaki → Tsuruga train, so do not route via the Hokuriku line.
  1. From Kinosaki Onsen Station, take the Limited Express Kinosaki (きのさき) direct to Kyoto Station. About 2h25. Reserved seats, buy at the station or a JR West machine.
  2. At Kyoto Station, follow the blue Tokaido Shinkansen signs and board a Nozomi toward Tokyo. About 2h15.
  3. Get off at Tokyo Station, then JR one stop back to Ueno (7 minutes, Suica) and your return hotel on the Ueno side.
  4. Total: about 5 hours moving, plan 6 with the transfer and kids.
Ticket: roughly ¥18,000-19,000 per adult all-in, reserved (Kinosaki express about ¥4,400 + Shinkansen about ¥14,200). Buy both legs together at Kinosaki Station in the morning, or on SmartEX for the Shinkansen leg.
Kids will be fried. Dinner near the hotel, nothing ambitious.
Wed Sep 2

Ueno → Narita T1 (departure)

NH12 leaves at 5:00 pm. Keep the morning boring and the airport run clean.

Keisei-Ueno Station entrance. This is where the Skyliner home leaves from.
  1. Walk or taxi (about ¥700) to Keisei-Ueno Station.
  2. Buy Skyliner tickets to Narita Airport Terminal 1 at the counter or blue machines, about ¥2,580 per adult. Or book on the Keisei site the night before.

  4. Timing: be moving from Ueno by 1:30-2:00 pm for the 5:00 pm flight. That lands you at the airport around 2:45-3:00 with margin.

    Suggested attire

    Late August / early September Japan is hot, humid, and air-conditioned indoors. Pack for sweat, walking, and quick laundry.

    Rule of thumb

    Light, breathable, washable

    Michigan humidity helps, but Japan still feels heavier in the city. Pack like everyone will sweat through one shirt a day, because they probably will.

    Adults
    Tops5–6 breathable tees or short-sleeve shirtsBottoms3 light shorts/pantsLayer1 thin hoodie or light long sleeveShoesBroken-in walking sneakers + sandalsExtrasHat, water bottle, rain layer, nicer dinner outfit
    Kids
    Tops6–8 shirts, because mess and sweat are guaranteedBottoms4 shorts / light pantsLayer1 light long sleeve eachShoesComfortable sneakers + easy sandalsExtrasHat, swimsuit, spare shirt in day bag, pajamas
    What not to pack

    Heavy denim, brand-new shoes, thick hoodies, and too many “just in case” outfits. Plan one laundry cycle instead.

      Transport

      Train plan

      Use the Tokaido Shinkansen for Tokyo → Osaka, then Kyoto → Tsuruga and the local Obama Line for Mihama. For the return, the clean route is Kinosaki → Tsuruga → Tokyo.

      Beach access

      Mihama reality check

      Mihama Station is not the beach. Plan on a short taxi or local ride to Suishohama, especially with kids and bags.

      Onsen booking

      Book the right bath

      For this family, book a ryokan with a private or family bath. That is the easiest version of the onsen experience with kids.

      Sister field notes

      Transit app

      Google Maps is the move

      Her #1 tip. It names the exact platform, car, and door for every train and she never got lost. Download offline maps for Tokyo, Osaka, and Kyoto before the flight.

      Dinner default

      Izakaya nights

      Basically a Japanese pub with food, and her frequent dinner answer. Order a spread of small plates; kids eat fine, zero ceremony. Look for lantern signs near any station.

      Konbini staples

      7-Eleven hits

      Her staples: curry bread from the hot case on the counter, the egg sando, and the smoothies. Also your ATM and ticket-kiosk backup everywhere on the route.

      Vending find

      Fresh OJ machines

      If you spot a fresh-squeezed orange juice vending machine, buy it. Squeezed in front of you. Her verdict: best OJ of her life.

      Bullet train rule

      Ekiben first, platform second

      Get to Tokyo Station early on Shinkansen days. Buy the ekiben before the platform hunt; the good boxes sell out and station navigation with kids takes longer than you think.

      Sushi spot TBD

      Local dining intel · via Eimi

      Review rule

      Google stars lie in Japan

      Her rule: Japanese Google reviews run low, so a low score does not mean bad. The local system is Tabelog. Check tabelog.com/en and the Tabelog Award list instead of Google.

      Quality mark

      The 百名店 sticker

      Hyaku Meiten means “100 quality stores,” Tabelog’s annual best-of list. Restaurants on it display a golden-brown 百名店 sticker by the door. If you pass one, it is usually great. No research needed.

      Safety net

      Kyoto is hard to get wrong

      Her note: Kyoto stacks the most Michelin stars per capita. Between that and the 百名店 sticker rule, pick places by vibe and queue length and you will eat well.
